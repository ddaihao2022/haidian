# Qinghe Slow-Mobility & Transit Interchange Corridor

## Design Basis and Source Register
This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. All design judgments are decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis.

## Three-Level Scope Framework
The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope around the Jing-Zhang heritage park, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Qinghe Slow-Mobility & Transit Interchange Corridor", uses the heritage park as the public-space spine, three key areas as mobility anchors, and a blue-green slow-mobility composite loop as the connector.

## Key-Area Detailed Design
The Qinghe TOD Core is positioned as a station-city integrated hub emphasizing four-quadrant walkability and slow-mobility gap mending. The Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone focuses on broken-link connectivity, greenways and non-motorized priority. The Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link focuses on near-campus interchange, community micro-circulation and green travel.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas
Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI walk navigation, transit crowd prediction, slow-mobility safety sandbox, edge-compute stations, international roadshow lobby, Qinghe low-carbon corridor, near-campus transfer street, data-element lounge, AI life-service street and a global AI activity-week route.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal
Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services
The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape
A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Qinghe and Xiaoyuehe corridors, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing
Six renewal projects (QH-01..QH-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Metrics, Recalculation and Compliance
Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, slow-coverage, transit coverage, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Risk, Copyright and Compliance
This package does not claim official approval, finalized regulatory control, land ownership or guaranteed implementation. All assets declare sources and licenses in `sources.json` and `report/copyright_statement.md`. The HTML pages load no remote scripts, tiles, fonts, iframes or APIs.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks international corridors built on "transit interchange + slow-mobility priority + blue-green spine", extracting strategies directly transferable to the Qinghe corridor. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Copenhagen Cycle Superhighways (Cykelsuperstier)
Since 2009 the Copenhagen region has built a metropolitan cycle superhighway network across 28 municipalities (planned 750 km, 45+ routes built), using a unified signage system, continuous right-of-way, transit-priority signals and a "service-corridor" standard that upgrades commuting cycling from point facilities to a regional corridor. Lesson for this package: the Qinghe blue-green slow composite loop must be organized as one continuous, signed, signal-priority corridor rather than piecemeal repairs; the Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone's broken-link connectivity is its first landing segment.

### Case 2: Grand Paris Express and "Paris Respire" slow priority
Paris anchors its "15-minute city" and car-free "Paris Respire" districts on RER / Grand Paris Express hubs, binding four-quadrant station walkability to neighborhood slow networks. Lesson: at the Qinghe TOD Core, four-quadrant walkability and rail interchange should treat "slow mobility the moment you exit the station" as the primary station-city interface, not merely a transfer.

### Case 3: Seoul Cheonggyecheon Restoration (2003)
Seoul demolished an elevated road and daylighted a 10.9 km urban stream, creating a through-center blue-green corridor with continuous riverside walking and cycling paths, making the blue line the spine that stitches public life and slow mobility. Lesson: the Qinghe blue line should be the spatial spine linking the Qinghe TOD Core, Xitucheng and Heqing Rd key areas, with blue-green and slow mobility sharing one corridor rather than two separate systems.

### Case 4: Rotterdam Centraal bicycle interchange (fietsenstalling, 2013)
Rotterdam Central Station pairs 10,000+ covered bicycle parking spaces with a "bicycle-priority" station approach, shifting the modal split of rail interchange away from cars toward non-motorized travel. Lesson: the Qinghe TOD Core should reserve scaled non-motorized parking and interchange waiting space as the physical landing point for the 92% transit-800m-coverage target.

### Case 5: Bogota TransMilenio and Avenida 7 ciclorrutas
Bogota organizes corridor-scale travel with a BRT spine plus 550+ km of protected bike network (ciclorrutas), where slow mobility holds an independent right-of-way and continuous signals. Lesson: broken-link connectivity in the Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone and the Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link should pursue protected, continuous slow right-of-way rather than mixing with motor traffic.

### Case 6: Amsterdam Zuidas bicycle-friendly rail interchange
Amsterdam's Zuidas business district organizes rail interchange around "bicycle-first" station access, with near-campus / near-office populations completing the last kilometer by slow mobility. Lesson: the Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link, serving Tsinghua and nearby campuses, should answer its "near-campus interchange" positioning with community micro-circulation and green-travel interchange.

## Logo & Visual Identity (VI)

### Naming system
The package's overall concept name is **"Qinghe Blue-Green Slow Loop"** (abbreviated **QSL**). The name maps directly onto the spatial structure of "blue-green corridor + slow composite loop + rail interchange" and avoids generic slogans.

### Identity principles
The mark uses a "loop" as its core visual language: one continuous ring interwoven from blue (rail), green (slow mobility) and cyan (blue line), with three nodes on the ring symbolizing the Qinghe TOD Core, Xitucheng and Heqing Rd key areas, and an open center representing enterable public space.

- Primary: Rail Blue `#2E6FB0` (rail interchange and trust)
- Secondary: Slow Green `#2ECC71` (slow mobility / non-motorized priority), Blue-Line Cyan `#1FA2A6` (Qinghe blue-green corridor)
- Accent: Interchange Orange `#E8833A` (interchange node / key-area marker), Neutral Grey `#5A6470` (base map and text)
- Typography: Chinese headings in Source Han Sans Bold; English headings in IBM Plex Sans / Inter; body in Source Han Sans Regular / IBM Plex Sans Regular.
- Applications: A0 board title bar, A3 booklet cover, HTML page header, station digital-screen corner mark.

### Wayfinding system
A three-tier wayfinding is set along the Qinghe corridor:
1. Tier-1 nodes (Qinghe TOD Core entrances): concept name + four-quadrant walkability index + QR code (links to the HTML visual page).
2. Tier-2 nodes (slow broken-links / intersections): directional guidance + slow-priority icon + real-time interchange-wait indicator.
3. Tier-3 markers (community micro-circulation paths): distance markers + accessibility hints + shared-bike parking points.

> Note: the Logo and VI above are conceptual; final vector files require a professional design team grounded in this positioning, and all fonts and images must have cleared-rights sources.

### Test & verification scenarios (>=3)

**TS-01 Slow broken-link detection accuracy**
- Goal: AI-detected broken links in the Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone (missing paths, mixed traffic, long crossing waits) vs planner field survey achieve overlap >= 85% and false-positive < 10%.
- Method: item-by-item comparison of the AI list vs the field list; log miss/false-positive types.
- Pass: overlap >= 85% and false-positive < 10%; every link has a layer anchor [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001].

**TS-02 Rail-interchange wait-time prediction**
- Goal: at the Qinghe TOD Core, "exit-interchange" walk/cycle time prediction error MAPE < 15%, P90 interchange walk-time estimate error < 3 min.
- Method: compare AI-predicted interchange time vs field samples (peak/off-peak); track error distribution.
- Pass: MAPE < 15%; P90 error <= 3 min; no unauthorized personal trajectories output.

**TS-03 Bilingual figure consistency**
- Goal: verify information equivalence of bilingual content (proposal.md <-> proposal.en.md, zh PNG <-> en PNG).
- Method: item-by-item comparison of 5 bilingual figure label translations and metric numeric consistency.
- Pass: all en-figure labels are valid English (not Chinese copies); numeric error = 0.

## Landmarks & Honor System

### Physical landmarks
Three themed landmarks are set along the Qinghe corridor, one per key area:

1. **Qinghe TOD "Interchange Ring"** (ground paving + LED ring): at the Qinghe TOD Core exit, a ground ring marks four-quadrant walkability and rail-interchange direction, with LED showing live interchange waits and slow-priority cues.
2. **Xitucheng "Broken-Link Bridge"** (slow over/under-pass): at the Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone broken point, an overpass or underpass reconnects slow paths severed by the ring road, with embedded slow-mileage counters on the deck.
3. **Hequing Rd "Community Interchange Pavilion"** (mobile service pod): at the Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link near-campus entrance, offering interchange booking, shared-bike parking and green-travel guidance.

### Digital honor wall
On the HTML visual page and at the Qinghe Blue-Green Life Festival, a "Slow-Mileage Honor Wall" shows:
- Green-travel mileage leaderboard (PIPL-anonymized aggregation, weekly/monthly)
- Broken-link report adoption leaderboard (citizens whose reports were adopted)
- Interchange-scene usage-frequency leaderboard (most-used navigation/interchange scenes)
- Partner enterprise and institution logo wall

> Note: the honor system's scoring algorithm, review and anti-abuse mechanism must be co-designed by multiple stakeholders in Phase 1.

## Cultural Narrative & Communications

### Narrative spine
The package's narrative revolves around **"mobility as public life"**:
1. **From railway to corridor**: Jing-Zhang Railway (1909) -> Jing-Zhang HSR Qinghe Station (2019) -> Qinghe Blue-Green Slow Loop (2026+); a century of transport infrastructure continuously carrying the city's public life.
2. **From commute to experience**: rail interchange is not only transfer but stitches slow mobility, blue-green and community activity into an everyday experiential corridor.
3. **Local-global mutual learning**: the Qinghe corridor learns from and with Copenhagen, Amsterdam and Bogota on slow-mobility / rail-interchange best practice.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; interchange-booking forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is "conclusion-first" (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (corridor-specific)
- **Older adults**: Qinghe TOD interchange flow offers large-type UI + voice guidance; offline touchpoints have volunteer assistance for transfers.
- **Persons with disabilities**: the Qinghe blue-green slow composite loop is fully accessible (ramp <= 1:12, continuous tactile paving, accessible crossings); slow navigation prioritizes reporting accessible paths.
- **Children & near-campus populations**: the Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link provides an independent, continuous, protected school-travel slow path separated from motor traffic.
- **Low-income groups**: all digital interchange services keep offline alternatives (paper guides, hotline) so lack of a smartphone is never exclusionary.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: interchange and slow-mobility data collect only trip-service-relevant aggregates (origin/destination zone, wait duration); no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from trip content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the interchange and slow services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
