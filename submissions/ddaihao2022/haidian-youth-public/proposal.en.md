# Haidian Youth-Friendly Third-Space Network

## Design Basis and Source Register
This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. All design judgments are decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis.

## Three-Level Scope Framework
The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope around the Jing-Zhang heritage park, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Haidian Youth-Friendly Third-Space Network", uses rail stations and university edges as anchors, three key areas as social nodes, and a third-space corridor as the connector.

## Key-Area Detailed Design
Wudaokou Youth Hub is positioned as a 24-hour learning-and-social node emphasizing transit interchange, shared study pods and book cafes. Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space focuses on roadshow atriums, open-source workshops and talent hostels. Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt centers on waterfront markets, sports fields and night lighting.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas
Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering youth community app, event recommendation, night-safety sandbox, edge-compute stations, open-source workshop booking, talent matchmaking, sports booking, night-wayfinding, community feedback wall and a campus-life route.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal
Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services
The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape
A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Qinghe and Xiaoyuehe corridors, integrating slow loops, sports, youth interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing
Six renewal projects (YF-01..YF-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Metrics, Recalculation and Compliance
Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, third-space count, night-vitality area, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Risk, Copyright and Compliance
This package does not claim official approval, finalized regulatory control, land ownership or guaranteed implementation. All assets declare sources and licenses in `sources.json` and `report/copyright_statement.md`. The HTML pages load no remote scripts, tiles, fonts, iframes or APIs.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package focuses on a "youth-friendly third-space network". Six real international cases are selected from public sources (no official partnership or endorsement implied); each yields one concrete "what this package borrows" lesson tied to Haidian's youth hubs, startup third-space and night-vitality belt.

### Case 1: Seoul Zero Gravity Zone (무중력지대, Republic of Korea)
Since 2019 Seoul has placed "무중력지대" (Zero Gravity Zone) youth culture spaces in all 25 districts, offering rent-free venues to youth groups who self-curate exhibitions, performances, markets and skill workshops, with the city handling only compliance and safety. The key lesson: "zero-cost space + youth self-operation + government stepping back". This package borrows: Wudaokou Youth Hub and Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt should keep a share of rent-free youth co-creation space run by rotating youth associations rather than fully outsourced to commerce.

### Case 2: Amsterdam Night Mayor (Nachtburgemeester, Netherlands)
Amsterdam created an independent "Night Mayor" in 2012 as a permanent liaison among city hall, venue operators, residents and nightlife participants, setting noise, lighting, evacuation and neighbourhood-communication norms. The lesson: "night-time economy needs a dedicated governance interface, not daytime rules reused". This package borrows: Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt should have a standing "night coordinator" role unifying waterfront markets, sports fields and night lighting around open-hours, noise boundaries and security coordination.

### Case 3: Tokyo Miyashita Park (宮下パーク, Japan)
Reopened in 2020, Miyashita Park stacks a rooftop park, youth street-retail, sports courts and night cultural events vertically in Shibuya's core, becoming a 24-hour stayable youth third-space. The lesson: "combine public space, commerce and youth culture vertically to extend dwell time". This package borrows: Wudaokou Youth Hub uses a vertical stack of "book café + shared study pods + rooftop activity court" to raise 24-hour accessibility and dwell time.

### Case 4: Barcelona Superblocks (Superilles, Spain)
Barcelona's Superblocks slow traffic inside merged blocks and return streets to walking, play and community life, measurably increasing public activity space in pilots. The lesson: "traffic calming is a lever to release third-spaces". This package borrows: the community slow-mobility network around the three key areas follows Superblock logic — shrink car footprint, expand sit-and-talk street corners.

### Case 5: Medellín Library Parks (Parques Biblioteca, Colombia)
Medellín built "Library Parks" in formerly marginal hillside communities, treating learning, culture and youth public life as social-integration infrastructure, markedly improving youth belonging. The lesson: "third-space is equity infrastructure, not a luxury". This package borrows: Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space and Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt embed free shared study pods and open bookshelves, open without distinction to nearby university and migrant youth.

### Case 6: London Night Czar & South Bank (United Kingdom)
London appointed a "Night Czar" in 2016 to coordinate night-economy strategy; South Bank programs free outdoor performances, markets and public art along the Thames into an all-ages, all-income urban living room. The lesson: "night vitality comes from public programming, not just extended opening hours". This package borrows: the annual activity system and Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt put "public programming" at the centre, using free open-air events to drive night-safety and local spend.

## Logo & Visual Identity (VI)

### Naming system
The overall concept is named **"YouthPost · Third-Space"** (「青驿·第三空间」). "Youth/青" denotes youth; "Post/驿" echoes the century-old Jing-Zhang railway station imagery, resonating with the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt; the English short form **YS-Post** signals "youth post + third-space network".

### Identity principles
The mark uses "connection and stay" as its core visual language:
- **Primary graphic**: one continuous folded line passing through three dots — the line is the Jing-Zhang third-space corridor; the three dots are Wudaokou Youth Hub, Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space and Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt.
- **Colour palette**:
  - Primary: Youth Cyan `#00B5C7` (energy and connection)
  - Secondary: Night Orange `#FF6B35` (night-vitality belt and warm social)
  - Accent: Intelligence Purple `#7C4DFF` (AI and open-source)
  - Neutral: Ink Blue `#1A2238` (structure and trust)
- **Typography**: Chinese headings use Source Han Sans Bold; English headings use Space Grotesk / Inter; body uses Source Han Sans Regular / Inter Regular.
- **Applications**: A0 board title bars, A3 booklet cover, HTML page header, digital-screen corner mark, youth-hub entrance signage.

### Wayfinding system
A three-tier wayfinding along the third-space corridor:
1. **Tier 1** (entrances of the three youth cores): package name + area map + QR code (links to HTML visualization and event sign-up).
2. **Tier 2** (rail stations, main intersections): directional guidance + youth-scenario icons (book café / roadshow / night market).
3. **Tier 3** (slow paths, green space, waterfront): distance markers + accessibility and night-safety tips.

### Test & verification scenarios (≥3)

**TS-01 Open-data read accuracy**
- Goal: verify metrics the youth dashboard reads from open sources (site_area_sqm, third_space_count, night_vitality_area) deviate < 5% from independent recomputation.
- Method: compare agent auto-read values vs manual review; log deviation rate and error types.
- Pass: all 10 core metrics deviate < 5%; no "false positives" (agent fabricating non-existent data).

**TS-02 Youth-feedback loop response time**
- Goal: average time from youth submitting feedback to "handled / added to agenda" < 7 working days.
- Method: simulate 50 feedback items (3 types: facility repair / activity proposal / data correction); trace full duration.
- Pass: P90 response ≤ 7 working days; 100% receive confirmation receipt.

**TS-03 Bilingual equivalence**
- Goal: verify information equivalence of bilingual content (proposal.md ↔ proposal.en.md, zh PNG ↔ en PNG).
- Method: item-by-item comparison of 5 bilingual figure label translations and metric value consistency.
- Pass: all en-figure labels are valid English (not Chinese copies); metric value error = 0.

## Cultural Narrative & Comms

### Narrative spine
The cultural narrative revolves around **"three connections"**:
1. **History ↔ youth**: Jing-Zhang Railway (1909) → Jing-Zhang HSR (2019) → YouthPost third-space network (2026+). A century-old railway station hosts the newest youth-public-life experiment.
2. **Technology ↔ life**: AI does not replace human interaction but amplifies youth public life (event recommendation, night safety, shared study). The "youth data dashboard" is a tool, not a decision-maker.
3. **Local ↔ global**: Haidian's university and youth-density advantage learns with global youth-city practices from Seoul, Amsterdam and London.

### International channels
- **English proposal.en.md + en figures**: ensure international reviewers and youth partners read without friction.
- **Annual Global Youth City Week**: invite overseas youth-city representatives (Seoul, Amsterdam, London, etc.) for offline/online exchange.
- **Open-source internationalization**: technical components (scenario-card templates, feedback-loop code, visualization frontend) released on GitHub under MIT/Apache to attract global youth contributors.

### Sustainability risks
- **Funding**: Phases 1–2 can rely on existing fiscal and youth-development funds; Phase 3 needs diversified revenue (sponsorship, event fees, space services).
- **Talent**: needs hybrid talent in AI, urban planning and youth work; market supply is limited.
- **Politics & night governance**: the night-vitality belt touches noise, operating hours and neighbourhood relations; a clear "permitted-activity boundary" is needed to avoid nuisance and safety risk.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA:
- **Visual**: HTML contrast ≥ 4.5:1; charts provide alt text; A3/A0 boards use large type (title ≥ 24pt, body ≥ 14pt).
- **Operation**: all interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets ≥ 44×44px; feedback form supports voice input.
- **Cognitive**: avoid jargon piles; "conclusion-first" key info; consistent multilingual terminology.

### Vulnerable-group care
- **Youth with disabilities**: three youth cores' slow loops fully accessible (ramp ≤ 1:12, continuous tactile path, elevator coverage); shared-study pods and event booking prioritise accessible routes.
- **Low-income / no-smartphone students**: all digital services keep an offline alternative (paper forms, hotline, on-site volunteers); no exclusion from feedback or event sign-up for lacking a smartphone.
- **Women and minors returning at night**: Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt provides continuous lighting, visible monitoring and one-touch help points; night-wayfinding prioritises staffed, well-lit routes.
- **Elderly (intergenerational sharing)**: youth hubs offer a "large-text + voice-guide" assisted mode, encouraging intergenerational use of public space.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: youth feedback and event sign-up collect only info directly relevant (location optional, contact optional/anonymous); no behaviour trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: feedback stored on domestic servers; identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limits**: data used only to improve the youth public space and governance described here; no sale, no commercial recommendation, no third-party sharing without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: youth may view all their submissions and status; may request deletion (except legal retention); may refuse automated AI decisions and demand human review.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually; report published.

> This section is guided by GDPR, PIPL and the Data Security Law; detailed rules need legal professionals in Phase 1.
